## Target: Accel Partners

  
  ## Who You're Presenting To

Accel is one of the most successful early-stage venture capital firms in the world. Founded in 1983 by Arthur Patterson and Jim Swartz, Accel has backed Facebook, Slack, Dropbox, Spotify, Atlassian, UiPath, Squarespace, Venmo, DJI, Etsy, Flipkart, and hundreds more iconic companies.

**The Accel Profile:**
- **Stage Focus**: Seed through growth stage (but strongest at Series A/B)
- **Geography**: US, Europe, India (truly global perspective)
- **Sectors**: Enterprise SaaS, consumer internet, fintech, developer tools, marketplace platforms
- **Check Size**: $500K (seed) to $100M+ (growth)
- **Portfolio**: 400+ companies across 6 continents

**What Makes Accel Different:**
Unlike a16z (thesis-driven, contrarian bets) or Sequoia (enduring category leaders), Accel is **product and execution-obsessed**. They're operators at heart—they want to see that your product works, customers love it, and you can scale it systematically.

**The Decision-Makers:**
- **Partners**: 20+ partners globally, each with operational backgrounds
- **Principals**: Experienced investors who lead deals and work hands-on with portfolio
- **Specialists**: Talent, marketing, finance experts who support portfolio companies

**The Accel Process:**
- First meeting: Product demo + metrics deep-dive (they'll use your product)
- Second meeting: Customer references (they'll call your customers)
- Third meeting: Go-to-market and economics (how you scale)
- Partner meeting: Full partnership evaluates the investment
- Decision timeline: 4-8 weeks from first meeting to term sheet (fast for top-tier VC)

## The Accel Philosophy: "Product-Market Fit + Execution Velocity"

### The Core Belief: Products Win Markets

While a16z asks "What's your contrarian insight?" and Sequoia asks "Will this endure for 20 years?", Accel asks:

**"Do customers love your product?"**

This seemingly simple question drives everything at Accel.

**What "Customer Love" Means:**

It's not about:
- ❌ Revenue (that's an output of customer love, not the input)
- ❌ Vanity metrics (downloads, signups)
- ❌ Marketing prowess (paid acquisition covering weak retention)

It's about:
- ✅ **Retention**: Do customers stay? (especially early cohorts)
- ✅ **Engagement**: Do customers use it frequently and deeply?
- ✅ **NPS (Net Promoter Score)**: Do customers recommend it to others?
- ✅ **Organic growth**: Does word-of-mouth drive acquisition?
- ✅ **Expansion**: Do customers increase usage over time?

**The Accel Heuristic:**
"If your product disappeared tomorrow, would customers be **very disappointed** or would they find an alternative in 5 minutes?"

If the answer is "very disappointed," you have product-market fit. If the answer is "they'd switch," you don't.

## The Accel Investment Framework

### 1. PRODUCT-MARKET FIT (The Non-Negotiable Foundation)

Accel will NOT invest without clear evidence of product-market fit. This is their #1 filter.

**How Accel Evaluates PMF:**

**A. Retention Curves (The Most Important Graph)**

Accel wants to see **cohort retention curves that flatten**.

**Good Retention (B2C):**
Month 0: 100% of users
Month 1: 60% retained
Month 3: 45% retained
Month 6: 40% retained
Month 12: 38% retained
**Why this works:** Curve flattens after month 3—you're retaining a core user base.

**Bad Retention (B2C):**
Month 0: 100% of users
Month 1: 40% retained
Month 3: 20% retained
Month 6: 8% retained
Month 12: 2% retained
**Why this fails:** Curve never flattens—you're leaking users constantly (no PMF).

**Good Retention (B2B SaaS):**
Year 0: 100% of customers
Year 1: 95% retained (5% annual churn)
Year 2: 90% retained
Year 3: 86% retained
**Why this works:** <10% annual churn is world-class for B2B SaaS.

**Bad Retention (B2B SaaS):**
Year 0: 100% of customers
Year 1: 75% retained (25% annual churn)
Year 2: 56% retained
Year 3: 42% retained
**Why this fails:** >20% churn means customers aren't getting value—no PMF.

**Accel's Standard:**
- **B2C**: Month 6 retention should be >30% (ideally >40%)
- **B2B**: Annual churn should be <10% (ideally <5%)
- **Enterprise**: Logo churn <5%, net revenue retention >110%

**Your Task:** If you have retention data, show the curves. If they flatten, you have PMF.

---

**B. Net Promoter Score (NPS)**

Accel uses NPS as a leading indicator of PMF and growth potential.

**How NPS Works:**
Ask customers: "How likely are you to recommend this product to a friend or colleague?" (0-10 scale)
- **Promoters** (9-10): Enthusiastic, will drive referrals
- **Passives** (7-8): Satisfied but not enthusiastic
- **Detractors** (0-6): Unhappy, might churn
- **NPS Score** = % Promoters - % Detractors

**Accel's NPS Benchmarks:**

**World-Class (Target This):**
- NPS >50: Exceptional (examples: Slack at launch was 60+, Dropbox was 55+)

**Good:**
- NPS 30-50: Strong product-market fit, room to improve

**Acceptable:**
- NPS 10-30: PMF exists but product needs work

**Red Flag:**
- NPS <10: Weak PMF, customers aren't thrilled

**Why Accel Cares:**
High NPS = organic growth = low CAC = better unit economics = more sustainable business.

**Your Task:** Survey your customers, calculate NPS, report it honestly. If it's low, explain your improvement plan.

---

**C. Engagement Metrics (How Deeply Do Customers Use It?)**

Accel wants to see **high-frequency, high-intensity usage**.

**For B2C Products:**
- **DAU/MAU Ratio**: Daily active users ÷ Monthly active users
  - ✅ Great: >40% (Facebook was 60%+, Instagram 50%+)
  - ✅ Good: 25-40% (solid engagement)
  - ❌ Weak: <20% (low-frequency product, harder to monetize)

- **Session Length**: How long do users spend per session?
  - Context matters (gaming = hours, fintech = minutes)
  - Compare to competitors in your category

- **Session Frequency**: How often do users return?
  - ✅ Daily: Habitual product (strong)
  - ✅ Weekly: Frequent need (good)
  - ❌ Monthly: Occasional use (weaker PMF)

**For B2B Products:**
- **Seats Deployed vs. Seats Paid**: Are people actually using what they bought?
  - ✅ >80% utilization: Real usage
  - ❌ <50% utilization: Shelfware (bought but not used)

- **Feature Adoption**: What % of users use core features?
  - ✅ >60% using core features: Product is essential
  - ❌ <30%: Product isn't solving the problem

- **Workflow Integration**: How deeply is your product embedded?
  - ✅ Multiple touchpoints per day
  - ✅ Critical path for customer's job (can't work without it)
  - ❌ Nice-to-have, used occasionally

**Examples of Strong Engagement:**

✅ **Slack (2014):**
- DAU/MAU: 52% (people use it almost daily)
- Average session time: 2+ hours/day (always open)
- Messages sent per user per day: 300+ (core value delivered)
- Result: Accel led Series C at $250M valuation (grew to $27B at IPO)

✅ **Atlassian (Jira, 2010):**
- 85% of paid seats actively used
- Average team uses Jira 10+ times per day
- Workflow integration: Critical for development teams (can't ship without it)
- Result: Accel backed early, company IPO'd at $4.4B (now $45B+)

**Your Task:** Show engagement data. Prove customers use your product frequently and deeply.

---

**D. Organic Growth (The "Magic" Metric)**

Accel loves products that grow **organically** (word-of-mouth, referrals, viral loops) rather than purely through paid acquisition.

**Why Organic Growth Matters:**
- Lower CAC (customer acquisition cost)
- More sustainable (not dependent on ad spend)
- Signal of PMF (people love it enough to tell others)

**Organic Growth Indicators:**

✅ **Viral Coefficient >0.5**
- For every user you acquire, they bring 0.5+ more users
- If >1.0, you have exponential organic growth (rare and powerful)
- Examples: Dropbox (referral program), Slack (team invites)

✅ **Word-of-Mouth Attribution >30%**
- Ask new customers: "How did you hear about us?"
- If >30% say "friend/colleague recommended," strong organic motion

✅ **Payback Period Improving**
- If CAC payback drops over time, organic word-of-mouth is reducing reliance on paid

✅ **Inbound Leads Growing**
- Website traffic, demo requests, free trial signups without ads
- Growing inbound = brand awareness + product reputation spreading

**Examples:**

✅ **Dropbox (2008):**
- Referral program: Give storage for inviting friends
- Viral coefficient: 0.6-0.7 (every user brought 0.6 more)
- 35% of signups came from referrals
- Result: Accel invested Series A, Dropbox scaled to 500M+ users

✅ **Slack (2014):**
- Teams invite colleagues to join workspace
- Once 1 team adopted, other teams at company followed
- 40% of new teams came from within existing customer companies
- Result: Organic growth engine drove $100M+ ARR in 2 years

**Your Task:** Quantify organic growth. Show viral loops, referrals, inbound motion. If it's all paid acquisition, explain your plan to build organic channels.

---

**E. Customer Testimonials (The Qualitative Signal)**

Accel will call your customers. They want to hear **enthusiasm**, not just satisfaction.

**What They're Listening For:**

✅ **"We can't live without it"**
- Product is mission-critical
- Switching would be painful

✅ **"It's 10x better than alternatives"**
- Clear differentiation
- Quantified improvement

✅ **"We've expanded usage across the company"**
- Landed in one team, spreading to others
- Shows product stickiness

✅ **"We're recommending it to everyone"**
- Customers are evangelists
- Word-of-mouth validation

**Red Flags:**

❌ **"It's fine, does the job"**
- Lukewarm response
- No enthusiasm = weak PMF

❌ **"We're still evaluating how to use it"**
- Months after purchase, not getting value
- Deployment issues

❌ **"We're comparing it to [competitor]"**
- Not committed
- Considering switching

**Your Task:** Offer to connect Accel with 3-5 customers. Prep customers to give honest feedback (but if they're not enthusiastic, you have a PMF problem, not a presentation problem).

### 2. EXECUTION VELOCITY (Can You Ship Fast?)

Accel strongly values **speed of execution**. They want founders who iterate rapidly, learn quickly, and out-execute competitors.

**Why Execution Speed Matters:**

In competitive markets, **the fastest team wins**.
- Faster iteration = more learning
- More learning = better product
- Better product = more customers
- More customers = more data = flywheel accelerates

**How Accel Measures Execution Velocity:**

**A. Time to MVP**
- How long from idea to first customer?
- ✅ Great: <3 months
- ✅ Good: 3-6 months
- ❌ Slow: >6 months (unless deep tech)

**Example:**

✅ **Slack (Stewart Butterfield, 2013):**
- Idea in June 2013
- Internal dogfooding in August 2013
- Preview release (beta) in November 2013
- Full launch in February 2014
- Result: 8 months from idea to launch, 15K users on day 1 (all organic)

---

**B. Deployment Cadence (How Often Do You Ship?)**

**Accel's Expectation:**
- **Weekly releases** (ideal for web/SaaS)
- **Bi-weekly releases** (acceptable)
- **Monthly releases** (slow, but okay for enterprise)
- **Quarterly releases** (too slow, you'll get out-executed)

**Why It Matters:**
- Faster shipping = more customer feedback
- More feedback = better product decisions
- Better decisions = faster path to PMF

**Example:**

✅ **Atlassian (early years):**
- Shipped Jira updates every 2 weeks
- Customer feedback incorporated within 1 sprint
- Competitors (old enterprise software) shipped every 6-12 months
- Result: Atlassian captured market by out-iterating incumbents

---

**C. Customer Feedback Loop Speed**

**The Question:** How fast do you go from customer feedback → product update → customer sees it?

✅ **Excellent: <1 week**
- Customer reports issue Monday → Fix deployed Friday

✅ **Good: 1-2 weeks**
- Typical for small teams

❌ **Slow: >1 month**
- Customers feel unheard, momentum stalls

**Your Task:** Show your deployment cadence. Give examples of customer feedback that became features. Demonstrate you're learning fast.

---

**D. Competitive Response Time**

**The Scenario:**
Competitor launches a feature. How fast can you respond?

**Accel's Expectation:**
- You should know what competitors are doing (monitoring)
- If a competitor launches something important, you should have a response plan
- Ideally, you're **ahead** of competitors, not reacting

**Example:**

✅ **Zoom (2013-2015):**
- WebEx, Skype, Google Hangouts had video conferencing
- Zoom shipped new features monthly while competitors shipped annually
- By 2015, Zoom had 40M users while out-shipping all incumbents
- Result: Accel invested (Zoom → $100B+ market cap)

### 3. GO-TO-MARKET (Can You Scale Systematically?)

Accel is deeply operational. They want to see **repeatable, scalable go-to-market motions**.

**The Question:** "If we give you $10M, can you predictably turn it into $30M in revenue?"

**A. Customer Acquisition: Can You Repeatably Find Customers?**

**Accel Evaluates:**

**For B2B SaaS:**
- What's your sales process?
- How long is the sales cycle?
- What's the close rate?
- What's the average deal size?
- Can you scale this (hire more reps = proportional revenue)?

**Repeatable B2B GTM (What Accel Looks For):**

✅ **Inbound-driven pipeline:**
- 50%+ of pipeline from inbound leads (content, SEO, product-led)
- Outbound is supplement, not primary

✅ **Short sales cycle:**
- <3 months for SMB
- <6 months for mid-market
- <12 months for enterprise

✅ **High close rate:**
- >25% close rate on qualified leads (shows strong value prop)

✅ **Scalable sales model:**
- Proof that hiring more AEs increases revenue proportionally
- Example: 1 AE carries $500K quota, you have 5 AEs = $2.5M capacity, you're hiring 5 more = $5M capacity

**Example:**

✅ **HubSpot (early years):**
- Inbound marketing drove leads (SEO + content)
- Inside sales team closed deals (30-60 day cycles)
- Close rate: 30%+ on qualified leads
- Average deal: $10K annual contract
- Scaled from 5 AEs to 50 AEs in 2 years, revenue scaled proportionally
- Result: Accel invested (HubSpot → $30B+ market cap)

**For Consumer:**
- What channels drive user acquisition?
- What's the CAC (customer acquisition cost)?
- What's the LTV (lifetime value)?
- Is CAC improving over time (better targeting, organic growth)?

**Repeatable Consumer GTM (What Accel Looks For):**

✅ **Multiple acquisition channels:**
- Not dependent on one channel (e.g., only Facebook ads)
- Diversified: paid social + SEO + referrals + partnerships

✅ **Improving unit economics:**
- CAC trending down (organic kicking in)
- LTV trending up (retention improving)
- LTV:CAC ratio >3:1 (ideally >5:1)

✅ **Proven paid channels:**
- If using paid ads, CAC payback <6 months
- ROAS (return on ad spend) >3:1

**Example:**

✅ **Spotify (2010):**
- Freemium model (viral distribution)
- Facebook integration (organic growth)
- Conversion rate: 25% of free users → paid within 12 months
- CAC: $5-10 (mostly organic)
- LTV: $120+ (10+ months average subscription)
- Result: Accel invested early (Spotify → $50B+ market cap)

**Your Task:** Map your go-to-market motion. Show it's repeatable. Prove you can scale it with capital.

---

**B. Sales Efficiency Metrics**

Accel is obsessed with **capital efficiency**. They want every dollar invested to generate predictable returns.

**Key Metrics:**

**Magic Number (SaaS):**
- Formula: (Net New ARR in Q) ÷ (Sales & Marketing Spend in Q-1)
- ✅ Excellent: >1.0 (every $1 spent generates $1+ ARR in next quarter)
- ✅ Good: 0.75-1.0
- ❌ Weak: <0.5 (spending too much to acquire revenue)

**CAC Payback Period:**
- How many months to recoup customer acquisition cost?
- ✅ Great: <6 months
- ✅ Good: 6-12 months
- ❌ Slow: >18 months (too long, cash inefficient)

**LTV:CAC Ratio:**
- Lifetime value ÷ Customer acquisition cost
- ✅ Great: >5:1
- ✅ Good: 3-5:1
- ❌ Weak: <3:1 (not enough margin)

**Example of World-Class Efficiency:**

✅ **Atlassian (product-led growth model):**
- CAC: $500 (mostly self-serve, no sales team early on)
- LTV: $25,000 (companies stay for years, expand usage)
- LTV:CAC: 50:1 (exceptional)
- Payback: 2 months
- Magic Number: 1.5+ (hyper-efficient)
- Result: Scaled to $1B+ revenue with minimal sales/marketing spend

**Your Task:** Calculate and report these metrics. If they're weak, explain why and your improvement roadmap.

### 4. GLOBAL POTENTIAL (Can This Work Beyond One Market?)

Unlike many US-focused VCs, Accel thinks **globally from day one**. They have offices in US, UK, India, and invest worldwide.

**The Question:** "Can this company win in Europe, Asia, Latin America—not just the US?"

**Why Global Matters:**

**Market Size Expansion:**
- US market alone might be $5B TAM
- Add Europe + Asia = $30B TAM
- Global = venture-scale outcome

**Competitive Moat:**
- If you're in 20 countries and a competitor is in 1, you have network effects, brand, and operational scale they can't match

**Portfolio Leverage:**
- Accel can help with European expansion (London office)
- Accel can help with India expansion (Bangalore office)
- Accel portfolio companies can share playbooks

**What Makes a Company "Global-Ready"?**

✅ **Product is not US-specific:**
- Software works in multiple languages (or can be localized)
- No regulatory barriers that limit geography
- Problem exists in multiple markets (not US-only pain point)

✅ **Business model translates:**
- Pricing works internationally (not US-dollar-dependent)
- Payment infrastructure works globally
- Unit economics hold in other markets

✅ **Team has global ambition:**
- Founders think internationally from day one
- Willing to travel, open offices, hire locally

**Examples:**

✅ **Spotify (Sweden → Global):**
- Founded in Stockholm, not Silicon Valley
- Accel invested when Spotify was Europe-only
- Saw potential for global music streaming (problem is universal)
- Helped Spotify expand to US, then globally
- Result: Now in 180+ countries

✅ **UiPath (Romania → Global):**
- Founded in Bucharest (Romania)
- Accel invested Series A when UiPath was mostly Europe
- Robotic process automation (RPA) is global need
- Helped UiPath expand to US, then Asia
- Result: IPO at $35B valuation

✅ **Flipkart (India):**
- Accel invested in Indian e-commerce (pre-Amazon India)
- Bet on India's internet growth trajectory
- Helped scale to market leader in India
- Result: Sold to Walmart for $16B

**Red Flags:**

❌ **"We're focused on the US only"**
- Limits TAM, limits defensibility
- Unless US market alone is massive (rare)

❌ **Product requires physical presence in every market**
- Hard to scale (logistics, regulations, local ops)
- Not software-leverage

❌ **Founders have never left the US**
- Lack of global perspective
- Will struggle with international expansion

**Your Task:** Explain global potential. If you're US-focused today, show awareness of international opportunity and eventual expansion plan.

## The Accel Presentation Strategy

### Opening: Product Demo (Not Slides)

**Unlike a16z or Sequoia, Accel wants to SEE your product first.**

**The Ideal Opening:**

**First 2 Minutes:**
"Hi, we're [Company]. We [one sentence]. Let me show you what we built. [Screen share → demo]"

**Then transition to:**
"That's what we do. Now let me show you why it's working: [traction metrics]"

**Why This Works:**
- Accel is product-focused—they want to experience it
- Seeing the product makes everything else concrete
- A great product demo is more convincing than 20 slides

**Demo Tips:**

✅ **Show the "aha moment":**
- The moment when value becomes obvious
- Example: Slack → send a message, get instant reply, see threads organize
- Example: Dropbox → drag file to folder, watch it sync instantly across devices

✅ **Use real data:**
- Don't use dummy data ("lorem ipsum")
- Show actual customer data (anonymized if needed)
- Makes it feel real, not vaporware

✅ **Keep it under 3 minutes:**
- Accel partners have short attention spans
- They'll interrupt with questions (good!)
- If demo is too long, you lose them

✅ **Have a "wow" feature:**
- Something that makes them go "oh, that's clever"
- Technical innovation, design detail, or unexpected capability

❌ **Don't show features no one uses:**
- Focus on core value prop, not every bell and whistle

❌ **Don't get lost in settings menus:**
- Keep it high-level, user-facing

**Your Task:** Design your presentation to START with a product demo. Make it compelling, fast, and focused on value delivery.

**The Accel Flow (Different from a16z/Sequoia):**

**1. Product Demo (2-3 min):**

## The Accel Questions (Prepare for These)

### 1. "Walk me through your product. Can I try it?"

**What they're really asking:** Is this product actually good?

**Your Response:**
- Offer to share a demo account or live demo
- Walk them through the core use case
- Highlight the "aha moment"

**Example:**
"Absolutely. I'll send you a login after this call. The key flow is: [describe 3-step process]. The aha moment is when [specific value appears]. Our users do this 10+ times per day on average."

---

### 2. "What's your retention curve?"

**What they're really asking:** Do customers actually stick around?

**Your Response:**
- Show cohort retention graph
- Point out where curve flattens
- Explain what drives retention

**Example:**
"Here's our retention by cohort. You can see all cohorts flatten around 38-40% after month 3. That means for every 100 users we acquire, 40 become long-term users. Our best cohort is [recent one] at 45%—we've improved onboarding, which is lifting retention."

---

### 3. "What's your NPS?"

**What they're really asking:** Do customers love you enough to recommend you?

**Your Response:**
- State your NPS honestly
- Provide context (sample size, when measured)
- Explain what you're doing to improve it

**Example:**
"Our NPS is 48 based on a survey of 200 customers last month. Promoters love our [specific feature]. Detractors cite [specific pain point]—we're addressing that in our Q2 roadmap with [specific fix]."

---

### 4. "How do you acquire customers?"

**What they're really asking:** Can you scale customer acquisition predictably?

**Your Response:**
- Break down acquisition channels
- Show CAC by channel
- Explain which channels you'll double down on

**Example:**
"50% inbound (SEO + content), 30% outbound (cold email + LinkedIn), 20% partnerships. Inbound CAC is $500, outbound is $2,000. We're doubling down on inbound—hiring content marketers to scale what's working. As inbound grows, blended CAC drops."

---

### 5. "What are your unit economics?"

**What they're really asking:** Does the business model work?

**Your Response:**
- State CAC, LTV, payback period, gross margin
- Show these improving over time (if they are)
- Explain path to profitability

**Example:**
"CAC is $1,200, LTV is $8,000, so LTV:CAC is 6.7:1. Payback is 6 months. Gross margin is 78%. As we add features, customers expand spend—LTV is growing 15% per year. At $20M ARR with current margins, we're profitable."

---

### 6. "What's your expansion strategy?"

**What they're really asking:** Can this work internationally?

**Your Response:**
- Explain geographic expansion plan
- Address localization needs
- Show awareness of international markets

**Example:**
"We're US-focused today but the problem exists globally. Our top inbound demand is from UK and Germany (25% of free trials). We're planning Europe expansion in Q4 2026—hiring a GM there, localizing the product to EU languages, and adapting pricing for purchasing power. Accel's London office would be invaluable for this."

---

### 7. "Can I talk to some of your customers?"

**What they're really asking:** Is this real? Do customers actually love it?

**Your Response:**
- Enthusiastically agree
- Offer 3-5 customer references
- Prep customers with context (but don't script them)

**Example:**
"Absolutely. I'll intro you to [Customer A at Company X], [Customer B at Company Y], and [Customer C at Company Z]. They're all heavy users—Customer A has expanded from 10 seats to 100 seats in 6 months. They can speak to the value we provide."

---

### 8. "What keeps you up at night?"

**What they're really asking:** Do you understand your risks?

**Your Response:**
- Acknowledge 2-3 real risks
- Explain mitigation strategies
- Show thoughtfulness, not fear

**Example:**
"Three things: (1) Scaling hiring—we need to 3x headcount without breaking culture. Mitigation: We're building a rigorous hiring process and using Accel's talent network. (2) Competition—[Big Company] could enter this space. Mitigation: We're building moats through data and integrations. (3) International expansion—different regulations per country. Mitigation: We're partnering with local experts."

## Common Mistakes When Pitching Accel

### Mistake 1: Leading with Market Size Instead of Product

❌ **Bad Opening:**
"The [industry] is a $50B market growing at 15% annually..."

✅ **Better Opening:**
"Let me show you what we built. [Demo for 2 minutes]. That's what we do. Now here's why customers love it: [traction metrics]."

**Why it matters:** Accel wants to see the product first. Market size can come later.

---

### Mistake 2: Vanity Metrics Instead of Retention

❌ **Bad:**
"We have 100,000 app downloads!"

✅ **Better:**
"We have 100,000 downloads, 20,000 are monthly active (20% conversion), and our month-3 retention is 42%. Here's the cohort curve..."

**Why it matters:** Accel filters out vanity metrics instantly. Show retention or engagement, not just top-of-funnel.

---

### Mistake 3: No Go-to-Market Clarity

❌ **Bad:**
"We're going to grow through partnerships and word-of-mouth."

✅ **Better:**
"We have three acquisition channels: inbound (50%), outbound (30%), partnerships (20%). Here's the CAC and close rate for each. We're scaling inbound because it has the best unit economics."

**Why it matters:** Accel needs to see a repeatable, scalable GTM motion.

---

### Mistake 4: Ignoring Global Potential

❌ **Bad:**
"We're focused on the US market only."

✅ **Better:**
"We're US-focused today, but 30% of our inbound demand is international. The problem exists globally. We're planning European expansion in 2026 with Accel's support."

**Why it matters:** Accel invests globally. If you're US-only forever, you limit TAM and Accel's value-add.

---

### Mistake 5: Defensiveness About Product Weaknesses

❌ **Bad:**
[Accel Partner]: "Your mobile app feels slow."
[Founder]: "We don't think it's slow. Our users haven't complained."

✅ **Better:**
[Accel Partner]: "Your mobile app feels slow."
[Founder]: "You're right—mobile is our weakest point. We're rewriting the rendering engine in Q2 to fix this. Early tests show 5x speed improvement."

**Why it matters:** Accel values coachability. Acknowledge problems, show solutions.

---

### Mistake 6: No Customer References Ready

❌ **Bad:**
[Accel]: "Can I talk to your customers?"
[Founder]: "Um, let me check with them and get back to you..."

✅ **Better:**
[Accel]: "Can I talk to your customers?"
[Founder]: "Absolutely. I'll intro you to three customers today via email. They're all power users and can speak to the value we provide."

**Why it matters:** Accel will call customers. Be ready with references proactively.

---

### Mistake 7: Slow Follow-Up

❌ **Bad:**
Meeting ends. Accel asks for data. Founder sends it 1 week later.

✅ **Better:**
Meeting ends. Accel asks for data. Founder sends it same day or next day.

**Why it matters:** Speed of follow-up signals execution velocity. Accel is testing you.

## Your Mission: Show Product-Market Fit + Execution Capability

When analyzing materials for an Accel pitch, prioritize:

**1. Product Quality:**
- Is this a product customers love?
- Can you demo it compellingly?
- What's the "aha moment"?

**2. Product-Market Fit Evidence:**
- Retention curves
- NPS scores
- Engagement metrics
- Organic growth signals

**3. Go-to-Market Motion:**
- How do you acquire customers?
- Is it repeatable and scalable?
- What are the unit economics?

**4. Execution Velocity:**
- How fast do you ship?
- Customer feedback loop speed?
- Evidence of rapid iteration?

**5. Global Potential:**
- Can this work beyond one market?
- What's the international expansion plan?

**6. Team:**
- Founder-market fit
- Complementary skills
- Coachability signals

**Design a narrative that shows:**
- We built something customers love (product + PMF)
- We can scale it systematically (GTM motion)
- We execute fast (velocity)
- We can win globally (ambition + plan)
- We're the right team to do it (credibility + coachability)

**Remember:** Accel is operator-focused. Show them you can execute, scale, and win.
