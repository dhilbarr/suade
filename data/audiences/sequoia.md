## Target: Sequoia Capital

## Who You're Presenting To

Sequoia Capital is the most storied venture capital firm in Silicon Valley. Founded in 1972 by Don Valentine, Sequoia has backed Apple, Atari, Cisco, Google, Oracle, PayPal, YouTube, WhatsApp, Airbnb, Stripe, Instagram, Zoom, DoorDash, Snowflake, and hundreds more category-defining companies.

**The Sequoia Track Record:**
- 20% of NASDAQ's total value traces back to Sequoia-backed companies
- More IPOs than any other VC firm in history
- Average fund returns: 3-5x+ over decades
- Portfolio company combined value: $3.3+ trillion

**What This Means:**
Sequoia doesn't need you. They have 1,000+ inbound pitches per week. Getting a meeting is hard. Getting a term sheet is harder. They're brutally selective because they can afford to be.

**The Decision-Makers:**
- **Partners**: Senior investment leaders, each with 10-20 years experience
- **Principals**: Rising investors who champion deals and work with portfolio companies
- **Domain Focus**: Partners specialize in sectors (enterprise, consumer, healthcare, fintech, crypto)

**The Partnership Dynamic:**
- Investment decisions made by partner consensus (not individual partners)
- You need to convince the partnership, not just one person
- Partners challenge each other—they want to stress-test your company
- If your champion can't defend you in partner meeting, you won't get funded

## The Sequoia Philosophy: "Enduring Companies"

### The Core Question: Will This Matter in 20 Years?

Sequoia isn't looking for quick flips or modest outcomes. They're building **enduring companies**—businesses that define categories and dominate markets for decades.

**What "Enduring" Means:**

**Not Enduring:**
- ❌ Features that incumbents can copy in 6 months
- ❌ Marketplace with no network effects or switching costs
- ❌ Products that are "nice to have" but not essential
- ❌ Companies that peak at $100M revenue
- ❌ Trends that fade (remember fidget spinners?)

**Enduring:**
- ✅ Infrastructure that becomes essential (Stripe, AWS, Snowflake)
- ✅ Platforms with network effects (Airbnb, WhatsApp, LinkedIn)
- ✅ Category creators that redefine markets (Google, Apple, Zoom)
- ✅ Mission-critical B2B tools (ServiceNow, Workday, Salesforce)
- ✅ Companies that compound advantages over time

**The Sequoia Litmus Test:**
"In 2045, will this company still be growing and relevant? Or will it have been disrupted, copied, or made obsolete?"

**Your Task:** Show that your company can endure—not just grow fast in the short term.

## The Sequoia Investment Framework

Sequoia has a famous framework they use to evaluate companies. Understanding this framework is critical to designing your narrative.

### 1. COMPANY PURPOSE (Why Does This Need to Exist?)

**The Opening Question:**
"Why does the world need this company?"

This isn't about vision statements or corporate platitudes. It's about: **What problem is so important that a company must exist to solve it?**

**Great Examples:**

✅ **Stripe (2010):**
"Seven lines of code to accept payments vs. two weeks of integration work. Developers are building the next generation of internet businesses, but payments infrastructure is stuck in 1999. Someone needs to fix this."

**Why this works:**
- Clear problem: Payments are too hard
- Clear constituency: Developers building internet businesses
- Clear necessity: Can't have internet economy without payment infrastructure

✅ **Airbnb (2008):**
"There are 100 million spare bedrooms sitting empty every night while travelers pay $200+ for soulless hotel rooms. Both sides lose. Someone needs to connect them."

**Why this works:**
- Massive waste identified: 100M empty rooms
- Both sides benefit: Hosts earn money, travelers save money
- Category creation: "Home-sharing" didn't exist before

✅ **Snowflake (2012):**
"Data warehouses are on-premise, expensive ($500K+ to set up), and can't scale. Companies are drowning in data but can't analyze it. Cloud data warehousing needs to exist."

**Why this works:**
- Technical barrier: On-premise is dead-end
- Economic barrier: $500K+ is prohibitive for most companies
- Timing: Cloud matured, data exploded—convergence creates necessity

**Bad Examples:**

❌ "We're building a platform to disrupt the X industry."
- Generic, no specific problem, no necessity argument

❌ "We're passionate about making people's lives better through technology."
- Platitude, not purpose

❌ "There's a big opportunity in Y market."
- Opportunity ≠ purpose

**The Sequoia Perspective:**
They want companies that *must* exist—where if you didn't build it, someone else would have to, because the problem is too important to ignore.

**Your Task:** Articulate why this company needs to exist. What breaks if it doesn't?

### 2. MARKET OPPORTUNITY (Are You Going After Something Big Enough?)

Sequoia wants **market leaders in massive markets**, not niche players in small markets.

**The Sequoia Market Criteria:**

**A. Market Size: $10B+ TAM (Minimum)**

Sequoia's portfolio average outcome is $5-10B+ exit. That only works if the total addressable market is huge.

**How Sequoia Evaluates Market Size:**

✅ **Bottom-Up (Preferred):**
"There are 500,000 restaurants in the US. Each spends $15,000/year on delivery infrastructure. That's $7.5B TAM. We're targeting the 50,000 high-volume restaurants first—$750M SAM."

**Why this works:**
- Specific count: 500K restaurants (verifiable)
- Specific spend: $15K/year (based on research)
- Math checks out: 500K × $15K = $7.5B
- Focused SAM: 50K restaurants = credible go-to-market

❌ **Top-Down (Weak):**
"The restaurant industry is $800B. We're going after 1% of that = $8B TAM."

**Why this fails:**
- No specificity about who pays you
- 1% assumption is arbitrary
- Doesn't show understanding of market structure

**B. Market Structure: Are You Creating a Category or Competing in One?**

Sequoia prefers **category creation** but will invest in **category leaders** in existing markets.

**Category Creation (Best):**
- You're defining a new market that didn't exist
- Examples: Airbnb (home-sharing), Stripe (developer payments), Snowflake (cloud data warehouse)
- Advantage: No direct competition initially, you set the rules
- Risk: Market education is expensive and slow

**Category Leadership (Good):**
- You're competing in existing market but winning decisively
- Examples: DoorDash (food delivery—Uber Eats existed), Zoom (video—WebEx existed)
- Advantage: Market is proven, just need to execute better
- Risk: Incumbents fight back, price competition

**Category Participation (Avoid):**
- You're the 3rd or 4th player in a crowded space
- Sequoia rarely invests here—they want #1, maybe #2
- Exception: If you have 10x better unit economics or different business model

**C. Market Timing: Why Now?**

Sequoia is obsessed with timing. Too early = you die before market arrives. Too late = incumbents own it.

**What Changed Recently? (Sequoia Looks For Inflection Points)**

**Technology Inflection:**
- ✅ "GPU costs dropped 90% in 3 years, making real-time AI inference affordable for the first time"
- ✅ "LLMs crossed the threshold where they can reliably handle complex reasoning—this enables autonomous agents"

**Regulatory Inflection:**
- ✅ "New EU data privacy laws (2024) require companies to anonymize data—existing tools can't do this"
- ✅ "FDA greenlit AI diagnostics without full clinical trials—time-to-market dropped from 5 years to 18 months"

**Behavioral Inflection:**
- ✅ "COVID moved 60% of B2B sales online—buyers now expect self-serve"
- ✅ "Gen Z uses Discord, not email—workplace communication is shifting async"

**Economic Inflection:**
- ✅ "Rising interest rates killed growth-at-all-costs—companies now need profitability tools"
- ✅ "Labor shortages made automation urgent—warehouses can't hire fast enough"

**Market Maturation Inflection:**
- ✅ "Cloud adoption hit 70% of enterprises—cloud-native security is now mandatory"
- ✅ "EVs hit 18% of new car sales—charging infrastructure is the new bottleneck"

**The Sequoia Question:**
"What changed in the last 12-24 months that makes this possible now when it wasn't before?"

**Your Task:** Quantify the market (bottom-up), explain your positioning (creator vs. leader), and identify the inflection point (why now?).

### 3. PRODUCT (Do You Have Something People Want?)

Sequoia evaluates products through three lenses: **quality**, **defensibility**, and **product-market fit**.

**A. Product Quality: Is This Actually Good?**

Sequoia partners will use your product if they can. They'll ask engineers to evaluate it. They'll talk to your customers.

**What "Good" Means:**

✅ **Functionality:**
- Does it work reliably?
- Is it fast?
- Is the UX intuitive?

✅ **Differentiation:**
- What's the "aha moment"?
- What can your product do that competitors can't?
- Is it 10x better or just 10% better?

**Example: Zoom vs. WebEx**
- WebEx: Required downloads, 8-click setup, buggy on Mac
- Zoom: One-click join, worked everywhere, HD quality
- Result: 10x better UX = market leadership

**B. Product Defensibility: Can This Be Copied?**

Sequoia asks: "If you succeed, what stops [big company] from copying you?"

**Types of Defensibility Sequoia Values:**

**Network Effects (Best):**
- Product gets better as more people use it
- Examples: Airbnb (more hosts → more guests → more hosts), LinkedIn (more profiles → more value)
- Sequoia loves this—it's compounding defensibility

**Data Moat (Strong):**
- Proprietary dataset improves product
- Examples: Stripe (fraud detection from billions of transactions), Google (search quality from billions of queries)
- Must be data that competitors can't easily get

**Technical Moat (Good):**
- Hard technical problem solved
- Examples: Snowflake (query performance on cloud), Databricks (unified analytics)
- Must be 2+ years ahead, not 6 months

**Integration Moat (Solid):**
- Deeply embedded in customer workflow
- Examples: Salesforce, Workday (ripping out breaks other systems)
- Switching costs are high

**Brand Moat (Weak Unless Dominant):**
- Sequoia doesn't bet on brand alone
- Exception: Consumer companies where brand IS the moat (Airbnb, Instagram)

**What's NOT a Moat:**
- ❌ "First-mover advantage" (easily erased)
- ❌ "Better UI" (subjective, easily copied)
- ❌ "Using AI" (everyone uses AI)
- ❌ "Our team" (teams change)

**C. Product-Market Fit: Do People Actually Want This?**

Sequoia's PMF Indicators:

**For B2C:**
- ✅ Organic growth (not paid acquisition)
- ✅ High engagement (DAU/MAU >40%)
- ✅ Strong retention (>60% at 30 days)
- ✅ Word-of-mouth spreading
- ✅ Users would be "very disappointed" if product went away (survey result >40%)

**For B2B:**
- ✅ Short sales cycles (<3 months)
- ✅ High close rates (>30%)
- ✅ Customers expanding usage (net revenue retention >120%)
- ✅ Low churn (<5% annually)
- ✅ Referrals from existing customers

**The Sequoia Heuristic:**
"Are customers pulling the product from you, or are you pushing it onto them?"

**Your Task:** Demonstrate product quality, explain your moat, and prove product-market fit.

### 4. BUSINESS MODEL (Is This Actually a Business?)

Sequoia is ruthlessly focused on **economics**. Can this company be profitable? What are the unit economics? How does it scale?

**The Sequoia Business Model Questions:**

**A. Revenue Model Clarity**

**You Must Answer:**
- How do you make money? (SaaS subscription? Transaction fee? Marketplace take rate? Usage-based?)
- Who pays? (End user? Business? Platform?)
- How much do they pay? (Pricing structure)
- Why do they pay? (What value justifies the price?)

**Strong Examples:**

✅ **Stripe:**
"We charge 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction. Developers pay this because integrating with banks directly would cost $50K+ in engineering time and 6 months. Our take rate drops their all-in cost by 40%."

✅ **Snowflake:**
"We charge for compute and storage separately. Customers pay only for what they use. A typical customer spends $50K/year, but can scale to $5M+ as data needs grow. Our largest customer pays $20M annually."

✅ **DoorDash:**
"We take 15-25% commission from restaurants (varies by tier), plus $5-7 delivery fee from consumers, plus Dashers pay their own costs (gig model). Average order is $35, we net $8-10."

**Bad Examples:**

❌ "We're building the platform first, we'll figure out monetization later."
- This is a death sentence at Sequoia

❌ "We have multiple revenue streams."
- Sequoia wants focus, not complexity

❌ "We'll monetize through data eventually."
- Vague, no validation

**B. Unit Economics (THE MOST IMPORTANT SECTION)**

Sequoia will not invest if unit economics don't work. Period.

**The Core Metrics:**

**For SaaS:**
- **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**: What does it cost to acquire a customer?
- **LTV (Lifetime Value)**: How much revenue does a customer generate over their lifetime?
- **LTV:CAC Ratio**: Must be >3:1 (ideally >5:1)
- **CAC Payback Period**: Must be <12 months (ideally <6 months)
- **Gross Margin**: Must be >70% (ideally >80%)
- **Net Revenue Retention**: Must be >100% (ideally >120%)

**Example of Good Unit Economics:**

✅ **Slack (2014):**
- CAC: $1,500 (mostly inbound, product-led growth)
- LTV: $12,000 (average customer stays 4 years at $3K/year)
- LTV:CAC = 8:1 (exceptional)
- Payback: 6 months
- Gross margin: 85%
- NRR: 140% (teams expand usage)

**Result:** Sequoia led Series C at $1.1B valuation (grew to $27B at IPO)

**For Marketplaces:**
- **Take Rate**: What % of GMV do you capture?
- **CAC**: Cost to acquire buyers/sellers
- **LTV**: Lifetime spend per user
- **Contribution Margin**: After platform costs, what's left?
- **Cross-Side Network Effects**: Do more sellers attract more buyers (and vice versa)?

**Example of Good Marketplace Economics:**

✅ **DoorDash (2013):**
- Take rate: 18-20% blended (restaurant commission + delivery fee)
- CAC: $30 per user (paid marketing + referrals)
- LTV: $600 (average user orders 40 times over 2 years)
- LTV:CAC = 20:1
- Contribution margin after delivery costs: 5-8% (tight but works at scale)

**Result:** Sequoia led Series B (DoorDash → $70B market cap at IPO)

**What Sequoia WON'T Accept:**

❌ **LTV:CAC < 3:1**
- You're spending too much to acquire customers
- Not sustainable

❌ **CAC Payback > 18 months**
- Takes too long to recoup acquisition cost
- High cash burn, slow growth

❌ **Gross Margin < 60% (for software)**
- Not scalable
- Looks like services business, not software

❌ **Negative Unit Economics with No Path to Improvement**
- "We'll make it up in volume" doesn't work
- Exception: Marketplace with strong network effects showing path to profitability

**C. Path to Profitability**

Sequoia wants to see: **When will this company be profitable? What needs to happen?**

**The Sequoia Expectation:**
- Not profitable today? That's okay for early-stage
- But must show clear path: "At $X revenue with Y% gross margin and Z% sales/marketing spend, we're profitable"

**Example:**

✅ "We're at $10M ARR with 75% gross margin. We're spending 80% of revenue on sales/marketing (40%) and R&D (40%). At $50M ARR, sales/marketing drops to 30% and R&D drops to 25%—we're profitable at that scale. We'll hit $50M in 24 months."

**Why this works:**
- Current state clear: $10M ARR, 75% GM
- Profitability math: At $50M, costs drop enough to be profitable
- Timeline: 24 months (credible)
- Logic: Sales efficiency improves with scale (typical SaaS pattern)

**D. Capital Efficiency**

Sequoia respects **capital efficiency**. They want companies that can achieve a lot with a little.

**Capital Efficiency Metrics:**

**Growth per Dollar Raised:**
- ✅ Good: $2-3 revenue per $1 raised
- ✅ Great: $5+ revenue per $1 raised
- ❌ Bad: <$1 revenue per $1 raised

**Example:**

✅ **WhatsApp:**
- Raised $60M total
- Reached 450M users
- Cost per user: $0.13
- Sold to Facebook for $19B
- Return: 316x

**Result:** Sequoia's single best investment ever (turned $60M into $3B+)

**Time to Revenue:**
- How fast can you go from $0 → $1M → $10M → $100M?
- Sequoia wants rapid scaling, not slow grinds

**Burn Multiple:**
- Dollars burned per dollar of net new ARR
- ✅ Good: <1.5x
- ✅ Great: <1.0x (default-alive)
- ❌ Bad: >3.0x

**Your Task:** Show business model clarity, prove unit economics work, demonstrate path to profitability, and show capital efficiency.

### 5. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE (Why Will You Win?)

Sequoia doesn't invest in crowded markets unless you have a decisive advantage. They want **category leaders**, not also-rans.

**The Sequoia Competitive Questions:**

**A. Who Else Is Doing This?**

Be honest. Sequoia will research your competitors. If you claim "no competitors," you lose credibility.

**The Right Answer:**
"There are X direct competitors and Y adjacent players. Here's how we're different: [specific mechanism]."

**Example:**

✅ **Zoom (2013 vs. WebEx, Skype, Google Hangouts):**
"Video conferencing is crowded. But every incumbent requires downloads, has complex setup, and performs poorly on mobile. We're one-click, no download, HD quality on any device. That's why we're winning—our NPS is 68 vs. industry average of 12."

**Why this works:**
- Acknowledges competition
- Specific differentiation (one-click, no download, mobile)
- Proof of superiority (NPS 68 vs. 12)

**B. Why Haven't Incumbents Done This?**

The "obvious question": If this is such a good idea, why didn't [Google/Microsoft/Amazon] do it already?

**Good Answers:**

**Innovator's Dilemma:**
✅ "Microsoft has $50B revenue from Office licenses. Moving to cloud subscription would cannibalize that. We have no legacy revenue to protect."

**Focus/Priorities:**
✅ "Google's priority is search ads (98% of revenue). They've tried social 5 times and failed—it's not core to their business. We're 100% focused on this."

**Technical Constraints:**
✅ "Incumbents are built on SQL databases that can't handle real-time graph queries. Rebuilding their entire stack would take 3+ years. We started with graph-native architecture."

**Culture Mismatch:**
✅ "Enterprise companies sell top-down with 6-month cycles. We're bottom-up, product-led growth. They can't change their sales culture."

**Bad Answers:**

❌ "They're too slow/bureaucratic."
- Dismissive, not specific

❌ "They don't understand the market."
- Underestimates smart people with resources

❌ "We'll be acquired before they compete."
- Not a strategy

**C. What's Your Sustainable Advantage?**

Sequoia wants to know: In 5 years, when you're successful, what stops competitors from copying you?

**This goes back to moats (covered in Product section).**

The key: **Compounding advantages**.

**Examples:**

✅ **Network effects:** The more users, the better it gets (Airbnb, LinkedIn)
✅ **Data advantages:** Proprietary data improves product (Stripe fraud detection)
✅ **Switching costs:** Deeply embedded in workflow (Salesforce, Workday)
✅ **Brand/community:** (Instagram, Reddit)
✅ **Economies of scale:** (Amazon, Walmart)

**Your Task:** Map the competitive landscape honestly, explain why incumbents are vulnerable, and articulate your sustainable advantage.

### 6. TEAM (Can You Execute?)

Sequoia bets on people as much as ideas. They ask: "Is this the team that can build a $10B company?"

**The Sequoia Team Evaluation:**

**A. Founder-Market Fit**

**The Question:** "Why are YOU uniquely suited to build this company?"

**What Sequoia Looks For:**

**Domain Expertise:**
- ✅ Lived the problem firsthand (former practitioner in the industry)
- ✅ Deep technical knowledge (if technical product)
- ✅ Insider understanding of customer needs

**Examples:**

✅ **Stripe (Patrick & John Collison):**
- Both were teenage programmers who struggled with payment integration
- Built payment systems for their first startups
- Understood developer pain intimately

✅ **Snowflake (Benoit Dageville, Thierry Cruanes):**
- Both spent 10+ years at Oracle building databases
- Knew exactly what was broken with existing data warehouses
- Had technical credibility in the space

✅ **DoorDash (Tony Xu):**
- Grew up in parents' Chinese restaurant
- Understood restaurant economics and operations
- Saw delivery pain firsthand

**B. Complementary Co-Founder Skills**

Sequoia prefers **2-3 co-founders** with complementary skills:

**Ideal Combo:**
- Technical co-founder (can build the product)
- Business/sales co-founder (can sell and scale)
- Optional: Product/design co-founder (can make it delightful)

**Red Flags:**
- Solo founder (high failure rate, no one to share burden)
- All-technical team (who's selling?)
- All-business team (who's building?)
- Too many co-founders (>4 = decision paralysis)

**C. Track Record**

**Sequoia Loves:**
- ✅ Previously built/sold a company
- ✅ Early employee at successful startup (saw hypergrowth)
- ✅ Led major product/engineering at top company (Google, Meta, etc.)
- ✅ Domain expert (10+ years in industry)

**Examples:**

✅ **Amplitude (Spenser Skates, Curtis Liu):**
- Both were early at Palantir, saw how data-driven companies operate
- Built internal analytics tools at their previous startup

✅ **Figma (Dylan Field, Evan Wallace):**
- Dylan interned at LinkedIn, Flipboard, O'Reilly—saw design workflows
- Evan built WebGL projects, knew browser rendering deeply

**Sequoia Accepts (But Less Preferred):**
- First-time founders with exceptional domain expertise
- Young founders with rare technical skill (e.g., AI researchers)

**D. Execution Evidence**

**Sequoia asks:** "What have you shipped? How fast do you move?"

**Proof Points:**
- ✅ Product launched in <6 months from idea
- ✅ Rapid iteration (shipping weekly/bi-weekly)
- ✅ Customer feedback loops (talking to users constantly)
- ✅ Metrics improving monthly

**Example:**

✅ **Airbnb (2008):**
Sequoia initially passed. Then saw:
- Founders went to NY, photographed listings themselves (hustle)
- Cereal box fundraising stunt (Obama O's, Cap'n McCain's—creativity)
- Y Combinator traction (usage growing)
- Sequoia came back and invested Series A

**What changed?** Evidence of relentless execution despite rejection.

**E. Coachability & Self-Awareness**

Sequoia provides hands-on support. They need founders who:
- Listen to feedback (not defensive)
- Admit what they don't know
- Seek advice from experts
- Are willing to pivot when needed

**Red Flags:**
- ❌ Know-it-all attitude
- ❌ Blame others for failures
- ❌ Unwilling to take advice
- ❌ Rigid about approach (won't pivot)

**Your Task:** Establish founder-market fit, show complementary team, demonstrate track record, prove execution capability, and signal coachability.

### 7. FINANCIALS (What's the Growth Trajectory?)

Sequoia is exceptionally data-driven. They want to see your numbers—past, present, and future.

**A. Historical Financials (If You Have Revenue)**

**What to Show:**
- Monthly revenue for last 12-24 months
- Gross margin trend
- Burn rate
- Key metrics (ARR, users, GMV, etc.)

**Sequoia Wants to See:**
- ✅ Growth acceleration (not deceleration)
- ✅ Improving unit economics
- ✅ Consistent execution (hitting milestones)

**Example of Strong Historical Trajectory:**

✅ **Snowflake Series B (2014):**
- Month 1: $100K ARR
- Month 6: $500K ARR (5x in 6 months)
- Month 12: $2M ARR (4x in next 6 months)
- Growth rate accelerating (5x → 4x → maintaining >3x)
- Gross margins improving: 40% → 55% → 65%

**Result:** Sequoia led $26M Series B at $225M valuation

**B. Forward Projections**

Sequoia wants **3 scenarios**:

**Conservative Case:**
- What you're confident you can achieve
- Based on current trajectory with modest improvements

**Base Case:**
- What you expect to achieve
- Assumes things go reasonably well

**Aggressive Case:**
- What's possible if everything goes right
- Ambitious but not delusional

**Example:**

**Conservative:**
- End of Year 1: $5M ARR (3x growth)
- End of Year 2: $12M ARR (2.4x growth)
- End of Year 3: $25M ARR (2.1x growth)

**Base Case:**
- End of Year 1: $8M ARR (4.8x growth)
- End of Year 2: $20M ARR (2.5x growth)
- End of Year 3: $50M ARR (2.5x growth)

**Aggressive:**
- End of Year 1: $12M ARR (7.2x growth)
- End of Year 2: $40M ARR (3.3x growth)
- End of Year 3: $120M ARR (3x growth)

**Why This Works:**
- Conservative is achievable (low risk)
- Base case is credible (reasonable assumptions)
- Aggressive shows upside (venture-scale outcome)
- All three show path to $100M+ ARR (Series C/IPO scale)

**C. Capital Requirements & Milestones**

**Sequoia Wants to Know:**
- How much capital do you need?
- What will this capital achieve?
- What de-risks the company?
- When will you need to raise again?

**Example:**

✅ "We're raising $15M Series A. This funds 18 months to reach $10M ARR and break-even on contribution margin. We'll hire 10 engineers, 5 sales reps, and 1 head of marketing. Key milestones: (1) Launch enterprise tier (Q2), (2) Expand to Europe (Q3), (3) Hit $1M MRR (Q4). At $10M ARR with proven unit economics, we'll raise Series B to scale further."

**Why this works:**
- Specific amount: $15M
- Specific timeline: 18 months
- Specific milestones: Enterprise tier, Europe, $1M MRR
- Next step clear: Series B at $10M ARR

**Your Task:** Show historical traction (if any), provide credible projections (3 scenarios), and explain capital needs with clear milestones.

## The Sequoia Presentation Strategy

### Opening: Company Purpose (Not Product Description)

**❌ Weak Opening:**
"Hi, we're [Company], and we've built a platform that helps businesses [generic benefit]."

**✅ Strong Opening:**
"[Big problem] affects [large constituency] and costs [$X billion/year]. Nobody has solved this because [reason]. That's why we exist."

**Example:**

✅ **WhatsApp (2009):**
"SMS costs $0.10-0.50 per message internationally and carriers make $20B/year on texting. But texting is just data—it should be free. We're replacing SMS with free internet-based messaging."

**Why this works:**
- Quantifies the problem: "$20B/year carrier revenue"
- Clear constituency: "international texters"
- Clear purpose: "Free messaging should exist"

---

### Narrative Arc: Purpose → Market → Product → Traction → Model → Team → Ask

**The Sequoia Flow:**

**1. Company Purpose (20 sec):**
Why this company needs to exist

**2. Market Opportunity (30 sec):**
How big this can be, why now

**3. Product (45 sec):**
What you built, how it works, why it's defensible

**4. Traction (45 sec):**
Proof it's working (metrics, customers, growth)

**5. Business Model (30 sec):**
How you make money, unit economics

**6. Competitive Advantage (20 sec):**
Why you'll win vs. incumbents and startups

**7. Team (20 sec):**
Why you're uniquely positioned to execute

**8. Financials (20 sec):**
Where you're going (3-year projections)

**9. The Ask (10 sec):**
How much, what it achieves

**Total: ~4 minutes** (longer than YC Demo Day because Sequoia meetings are typically 60+ minutes with Q&A)

### Tone & Style for Sequoia

**Sequoia Values:**

**Substance Over Style:**
- They don't care about slick design or fancy animations
- They care about: Does the business work? Can it scale? Will it endure?

**Data Over Hype:**
- Show metrics, growth rates, unit economics
- Don't make unsubstantiated claims

**Honesty Over Spin:**
- Acknowledge risks and challenges
- Don't hide weaknesses—explain how you'll overcome them

**Long-Term Over Short-Term:**
- Show 10-year vision, not just next quarter
- Explain how advantages compound over time

**Execution Over Ideas:**
- Ideas are cheap; execution is everything
- Show what you've already built/shipped

**Voice Examples:**

✅ "We're at $5M ARR with 75% gross margins. Unit economics work: CAC is $1,200, LTV is $8,000, payback is 5 months. We're burning $300K/month and will be default-alive at $15M ARR in 18 months. Here's how we get there..."

✅ "The biggest risk is [Big Company] entering this space. But they've tried twice and failed because their legacy infrastructure can't support real-time processing. We're 2 years ahead technically, and switching costs are high once customers integrate our API."

✅ "Our product isn't perfect yet. We have bugs in the enterprise tier and our mobile app is basic. But we're shipping updates every 2 weeks and customer retention is 95%—they're willing to tolerate rough edges because we solve a critical problem."

❌ "We're revolutionizing the industry with cutting-edge AI and blockchain synergies to disrupt the paradigm."

## The Sequoia Questions (Prepare for These)

Sequoia partners will challenge you. Here are the classic Sequoia questions:

### 1. "Why will you win?"
**What they're really asking:** What's your unfair advantage?

**Good Answer:**
"We have three compounding advantages: (1) Network effects—every restaurant we add makes the platform more valuable for customers; (2) Data moat—we've processed 10M orders, giving us fraud detection accuracy competitors can't match; (3) Brand—we're the #1 app for food delivery in our market with 4.7 stars from 500K reviews."

---

### 2. "Why hasn't [incumbent] done this?"
**What they're really asking:** What's the barrier to copycats?

**Good Answer:**
"[Incumbent] generates $50B from [legacy business]. Disrupting that would cannibalize revenue. We have no legacy to protect. Plus, their culture is enterprise top-down sales; we're product-led growth. They can't change their DNA."

---

### 3. "What keeps you up at night?"
**What they're really asking:** Do you understand your risks?

**Good Answer:**
"Three things: (1) Scaling hiring—we need to triple headcount without diluting culture; (2) International expansion—regulations vary by country; (3) [Big Company] could enter this space. Our mitigation: (1) We're building a rigorous hiring process; (2) We're partnering with local experts; (3) We're building moats through data and integrations that take 18+ months to replicate."

---

### 4. "How do you think about pricing?"
**What they're really asking:** Do you understand your value proposition and customer economics?

**Good Answer:**
"We charge $5K/year per seat. Our customers save $50K/year on [alternative cost]. That's 10x ROI, so price isn't the objection—it's proving we deliver that ROI. We've tested pricing from $3K to $10K; at $5K we maximize volume without resistance. We'll raise prices as we add enterprise features."

---

### 5. "What happens when you're at $100M revenue?"
**What they're really asking:** Can you think 5+ years ahead?

**Good Answer:**
"At $100M revenue, we'll have 1,000 enterprise customers and 50,000 SMB customers. We'll be the category leader with 40% market share. Our focus shifts from acquiring customers to expanding wallet share—upselling adjacent products (analytics, integrations, white-label). We'll also move upmarket into global enterprises. Margins improve from 70% to 80% as we leverage infrastructure."

---

### 6. "Why do you need this much capital?"
**What they're really asking:** Are you capital-efficient?

**Good Answer:**
"We're raising $20M to fund 24 months. $8M goes to engineering (doubling team to build enterprise features), $7M to sales/marketing (hiring 10 AEs to penetrate F500), $5M to operations/G&A. This gets us from $10M ARR to $50M ARR—at that scale, we're Series B ready and potentially profitable. We could raise less ($12-15M) but that only funds 15 months and we'd be raising again before achieving key milestones."

---

### 7. "Walk me through the customer journey"
**What they're really asking:** Do you understand your own GTM motion?

**Good Answer:**
"Customer finds us through organic search or referral. They sign up for free trial (takes 2 minutes). Within 24 hours, they get onboarding email sequence. 40% activate (connect their data) in first week. Of those who activate, 65% convert to paid ($200/month) within 30 days. Average time from signup to paid: 18 days. After 90 days, 85% are still active. After 1 year, they've expanded to $400/month on average. CAC is $300 (mostly inbound), LTV is $4,800 (24-month average tenure × $200/month)."

## Common Mistakes When Pitching Sequoia

### Mistake 1: Thinking Small
❌ **Example:** "We can be a $50M revenue company"
✅ **Fix:** "We're building the infrastructure for [category]—this is a path to $1B+ revenue"

**Why it matters:** Sequoia wants enduring, category-defining companies. If you think small, you're not a fit.

---

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Unit Economics
❌ **Example:** "We're focused on growth; we'll figure out margins later"
✅ **Fix:** "We're at 65% gross margin today, targeting 75% at scale. Here's the path..."

**Why it matters:** Sequoia won't invest in businesses with broken economics.

---

### Mistake 3: Being Evasive About Competition
❌ **Example:** "We don't really have competitors" or "Our competitors are irrelevant"
✅ **Fix:** "There are 3 direct competitors. Here's how we differentiate: [specific mechanisms]. We're winning because [proof]."

**Why it matters:** Denying competition makes you look naive. Acknowledging it shows market awareness.

---

### Mistake 4: No Clear Moat
❌ **Example:** "We'll build network effects eventually"
✅ **Fix:** "We have network effects today: merchants with more reviews attract more customers; more customers attract more merchants. We're seeing 15% month-over-month growth on both sides."

**Why it matters:** "Eventually" isn't a strategy. Show it's working now or explain the specific mechanism.

---

### Mistake 5: Weak "Why Now?"
❌ **Example:** "The market is growing"
✅ **Fix:** "Three things changed in 2024: (1) GPT-4 crossed the accuracy threshold for medical use, (2) New FDA rules allow AI diagnostics, (3) Telehealth adoption hit 60%. This convergence creates the opportunity."

**Why it matters:** Sequoia wants to invest at inflection points, not slow markets.

---

### Mistake 6: Unclear Founder-Market Fit
❌ **Example:** "We're passionate about solving this problem"
✅ **Fix:** "I spent 10 years as a doctor and saw this problem daily. I built internal tools at [hospital] that saved 2 hours/day per physician. This product is the commercialized version."

**Why it matters:** Passion is nice; domain expertise is essential.

---

### Mistake 7: Unrealistic Projections
❌ **Example:** "We'll be at $100M ARR in 2 years" (currently at $1M ARR)
✅ **Fix:** "Conservative: $10M in 2 years (5x annually). Base: $20M (7x then 3x). Aggressive: $40M (10x then 4x). Here are the assumptions..."

**Why it matters:** Sequoia knows the math. Unrealistic projections destroy credibility.

## Your Mission: Design for Enduring Companies

When analyzing materials for a Sequoia pitch, ask:

**1. Purpose:** Why must this company exist?

**2. Market:** Is this big enough to be a $10B+ company?

**3. Product:** Is this defensible? Does it have PMF?

**4. Business Model:** Do the unit economics work? Path to profitability?

**5. Competition:** Why will this company win and keep winning?

**6. Team:** Can these founders build an enduring company?

**7. Financials:** What's the growth trajectory? Capital needs?

**Design a narrative that shows:**
- This company will matter in 20 years (enduring)
- This is a category leader (market dominance)
- The economics work (sustainable business)
- The team can execute (proven capability)

**Remember:** Sequoia is selecting the next Google, Stripe, or Airbnb. Show them why you're that company.
